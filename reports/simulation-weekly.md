# Simulated use of the book: page turns and ribbons (experiment: book-weekly.pdf)

Generated by `python -m lh simulate --variant weekly` on 2026-10-15 from the book as built (`book-weekly.pdf`, 3,409 pages) and the database. Do not edit: re-run the command.

**Experimental edition: the psalms and hymns of the four-week cycle printed at each hour.**

## What was simulated

**The book as printed now:**
- the **Ordinary card** (`book/out/card.pdf`, B-14) is in hand, so its texts need no page;
- each **Compline form is printed whole** (B-15);
- the **ribbons are assigned by use** (B-16): 1 Saints; 2 Commons; 3 Ordinary: Compline, Daytime Prayer; 4 Proper of Time: the hour; 5 Proper of Time: the week; 6 Hymns; 7 Psalter; 8 Psalter, Canticles; 9 Readings.

**The hours.** Each day the reader says:
- the Invitatory;
- the Office of Readings;
- Lauds;
- Daytime Prayer (Sext);
- Vespers, which is First Vespers of the next day when that day is a Sunday or a solemnity;
- Compline.

**Where each text comes from.**
- Every text is found on the page it is printed on, via the LaTeX labels of the last build.
- Invisible labels give where each hour begins (`hh:`) and where a text is printed again (`cp:`, as in whole Compline).
- Each hour starts at its heading, then every text is visited in order.
- Memorials take the weekday's psalms; feasts fill gaps from their Common.

**The calendar** is the General Roman Calendar (`lh/sim/calendar.py`), simplified:
- universal dates (Epiphany on 6 January, Ascension and Corpus Christi on Thursday);
- no transfers;
- obligatory memorials only (optional ones in one variant run).

**Each change of spread is classified as:**
- **turn**: to the next or previous spread (~1.5 s);
- **ribbon**: opening at a ribbon (~2.5 s);
- **look-up**: finding the page by its number (~8.0 s).

**Laying a ribbon** costs ~3.0 s. These times are rough guesses; the counts are the result.

**Ribbon policies:**
- *by use* and *by section*: each ribbon keeps a part of the book. The reader moves a part's ribbon to a new place only if that place will be needed again, today or tomorrow, before the place it marks now.
- *free*: any ribbon goes anywhere, by the best possible rule (Belady). This is the best these ribbons can do.
- *none*: no ribbons at all.

Ribbons stay where they are from one day to the next.

## Runs and timing

**Phase 1 (trial):** 10 random days, timed.
- 10 ms per simulated day.
- 7 s of setup.

**Phase 2:** every day of 28 consecutive liturgical years from Advent 2026 (a full cycle of weekdays against dates).
- 40,918 simulated days in 3.2 min.

**Phase 3:** 7 experiments in 0.6 min.

**Phase 4:** 3 reader-profile runs in 1.4 min.

**Total:** 5.2 min of a 90-min budget. Every planned run finished.

## What changed, and proposals

### Done: card, whole Compline, ribbons by use (B-14…B-16)

- **Combined:** without the card and with ribbons by section, the book needs 27.5 look-ups and 5.9 min a day. As printed now: 13.7 and 3.3 min.
- **Ribbons:** with the same card and Compline, the previous section-by-section scheme needs 15.7 look-ups, against 13.7 by use. The best possible placement (free) would need 11.4.
- **Compline:** whole Compline added 24 pages.

### Proposals (every text kept; ordered by time saved per page added)

1. **Thumb-index tabs on the fore-edge**, one per part (Saints, Commons, Ordinary, Proper of Time, Hymns, Psalter, Readings), as in many bibles.
   - The counts stay the same, but each look-up starts from the right part of the book.
   - At ~3 s less per look-up: about 0.7 min a day.
   - No pages added; printed as bleed tabs.
