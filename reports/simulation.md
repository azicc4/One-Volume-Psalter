# Simulated use of the book: page turns and ribbons

Generated by `python -m lh simulate` on 2026-10-15 from the book as built (`book.pdf`, 2,787 pages) and the database. Do not edit: re-run the command.

## What was simulated

**The book as printed now:**
- the **Ordinary card** (`book/out/card.pdf`, B-14) is in hand, so its texts need no page;
- each **Compline form is printed whole** (B-15);
- the **ribbons are assigned by use** (B-16): 1 Saints; 2 Commons; 3 Ordinary: Compline, Daytime Prayer; 4 Proper of Time: the four-week cycle; 5 Proper of Time: the week, the season's day; 6 Hymns; 7 Psalter; 8 Psalter, Canticles; 9 Readings.

**The hours.** Each day the reader says:
- the Invitatory;
- the Office of Readings;
- Lauds;
- Daytime Prayer (Sext);
- Vespers, which is First Vespers of the next day when that day is a Sunday or a solemnity;
- Compline.

**Where each text comes from.**
- Every text is found on the page it is printed on, via the LaTeX labels of the last build.
- Invisible labels give where each hour begins (`hh:`) and where a text is printed again (`cp:`, as in whole Compline).
- Each hour starts at its heading, then every text is visited in order.
- Memorials take the weekday's psalms; feasts fill gaps from their Common.

**The calendar** is the General Roman Calendar (`lh/sim/calendar.py`), simplified:
- universal dates (Epiphany on 6 January, Ascension and Corpus Christi on Thursday);
- no transfers;
- obligatory memorials only (optional ones in one variant run).

**Each change of spread is classified as:**
- **turn**: to the next or previous spread (~1.5 s);
- **ribbon**: opening at a ribbon (~2.5 s);
- **look-up**: finding the page by its number (~8.0 s).

**Laying a ribbon** costs ~3.0 s. These times are rough guesses; the counts are the result.

**Ribbon policies:**
- *by use* and *by section*: each ribbon keeps a part of the book. The reader moves a part's ribbon to a new place only if that place will be needed again, today or tomorrow, before the place it marks now.
- *free*: any ribbon goes anywhere, by the best possible rule (Belady). This is the best these ribbons can do.
- *none*: no ribbons at all.

Ribbons stay where they are from one day to the next.

## Runs and timing

**Phase 1 (trial):** 10 random days, timed.
- 14 ms per simulated day.
- 4 s of setup.

**Phase 2:** every day of 28 consecutive liturgical years from Advent 2026 (a full cycle of weekdays against dates).
- 40,918 simulated days in 3.0 min.

**Phase 3:** 13 experiments in 1.7 min.

**Phase 4:** 6 reader-profile runs in 2.1 min.

**Total:** 6.7 min of a 90-min budget. Every planned run finished.

## What changed, and proposals

### Done: card, whole Compline, ribbons by use (B-14…B-16)

- **Combined:** without the card and with ribbons by section, the book needs 29.0 look-ups and 6.3 min a day. As printed now: 17.3 and 4.1 min.
- **Ribbons:** with the same card and Compline, the previous section-by-section scheme needs 18.2 look-ups, against 17.3 by use. The best possible placement (free) would need 14.3.
- **Compline:** whole Compline added 24 pages.

### Proposals (every text kept; ordered by time saved per page added)

1. **Psalmody blocks: the Psalter in the order of the four-week cycle, with each hour's antiphons beside its psalms.**
   - Saves 0.6 min (15 %) a day; 15.3 look-ups, 33.9 moves.
   - **No text is added:** the psalms and canticles are reordered (each printed once at its first use, later uses referring to it), and the antiphons move from the grid into the Psalter.
   - **Nothing is lost:** psalms the cycle does not use (or only partly uses) follow in biblical order, so every verse is still printed.
   - **Trade-off:** biblical order gives way to the cycle. An index by psalm number and the margin notes of uses keep every psalm findable.
   - **Effect:** the grid page of an hour keeps the hymn, reading, responsory, intercessions and prayer, and the antiphon ↔ psalm turning disappears.

2. **The Psalter in cycle order only (antiphons stay in the grid).**
   - Saves 0.0 min (1 %) a day.
   - Most of the gain of the psalmody blocks comes from putting the antiphons with the psalms, not from the order.

3. **Psalms and hymns printed at each hour of the four-week cycle (branch `exp/weekly-psalter`).**
   - Saves 1.8 min (45 %) a day; 9.2 look-ups.
   - Costs about +334 pages (estimate; the branch measures the real count).
   - The Psalter in biblical order stays as it is.

4. **Psalmody blocks + hymns at each hour.**
   - Saves 0.9 min (22 %) a day for about +128 pages.
   - A middle way between the first proposal and the weekly psalter.

5. **Hymns alone at each hour.**
   - Saves only 0.4 min (10 %) for about +128 pages.
   - Hymns repeat across days, so a ribbon (7) already covers much of their turning.
   - Not recommended alone.

6. **Thumb-index tabs on the fore-edge**, one per part (Saints, Commons, Ordinary, Proper of Time, Hymns, Psalter, Readings), as in many bibles.
   - The counts stay the same, but each look-up starts from the right part of the book.
   - At ~3 s less per look-up: about 0.9 min a day.
   - No pages added; printed as bleed tabs.
