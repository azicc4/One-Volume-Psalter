# Simulated use of the book: page turns and ribbons

Generated by `python -m lh simulate` on 2026-10-14 from the book as built (2,841 pages) and the database. Do not edit: re-run the command.

## What was simulated

**The reader.** A reader prays the hours of each day from this one volume, using its ten ribbons:
1 Saints, 2 Commons, 3 Ordinary, 4 Proper of Time, 5 Hymns, 6–9 Psalter 1–4, 10 Readings.

**The hours.** Each day the reader says:
- the Invitatory;
- the Office of Readings;
- Lauds;
- Daytime Prayer (Sext);
- Vespers, which is First Vespers of the next day when that day is a Sunday or a solemnity;
- Compline.

**Where each text comes from.**
- Every text is taken from the page it is printed on in this book, via the LaTeX labels of the last build. 1,503 invisible labels at the hour headings give where each hour begins.
- Each hour starts at its heading. Every text is then visited in order: antiphon, psalm, antiphon again, reading, and so on.
- A memorial takes the weekday's psalms and its own proper texts; anything else comes from its Common.
- Feasts fill gaps from their Common; Lauds and Vespers of a feast use the psalms of Sunday Week I.

**The calendar** is the General Roman Calendar (`lh/sim/calendar.py`), simplified:
- universal dates (Epiphany on 6 January, Ascension and Corpus Christi on Thursday);
- no transfers;
- obligatory memorials only (optional ones in one variant run);
- memorials in Lent and on 17–24 December are commemorations only.

**Each change of spread is classified as:**
- **turn**: to the next or previous spread (~1.5 s);
- **ribbon**: opening the book at a ribbon lying in the spread needed (~2.5 s);
- **look-up**: finding the page by its number (~8.0 s).

**Laying a ribbon** costs ~3.0 s. These times are rough guesses; the counts are the result.

**Ribbon policies:**
- *by section*: the book's design, each ribbon keeping its section. The reader moves a section's ribbon to a new place only if that place will be needed again, today or tomorrow, before the old one.
- *free*: any ribbon can go anywhere, placed by the best possible rule (Belady). This is the best ten ribbons can do.
- *none*: no ribbons at all.

Ribbons stay where they are from one day to the next.

## Runs and timing

**Phase 1 (trial):** 10 random days, timed.
- 50 ms per simulated day (all six hours).
- 10 s of setup: the book generator, labels and calendar.

**Phase 2:** every day of 28 consecutive liturgical years, from Advent 2026. 28 years is a full cycle of weekdays against dates.
- Runs: *by section*, *free*, *none*, and *by section* with optional memorials kept.
- 40,918 simulated days in 8.3 min.

**Phase 3:** 12 layout and ribbon experiments over the same dates, in 5.9 min.

**Phase 4:** 6 reader-profile runs, in 6.1 min.

**Total:** 20.2 min of a 90-min budget. Every planned run finished, so the rest of the budget was not needed.

## Findings

**1. The Ordinary is the biggest single cost.**
- Its fixed parts are on four different spreads: the opening (p. 674), the Gospel canticles (pp. 679, 681), the Pater noster and dismissals (pp. 682–683), and the Compline forms (pp. 685–691).
- The one Ordinary ribbon is moved about 8 times a day.
- A loose **Ordinary card** removes about 11 look-ups and 19 moves a day (2.2 min). It costs no pages.
- The card holds the opening verses, Benedictus, Magnificat, Nunc dimittis, Psalm 95, Pater noster, dismissals, blessing and Te Deum, as many breviaries supply. A ribbon card or the endpapers would also do.

**2. Compline should be self-contained.**
- Printing each weekly form whole (its psalm and the parts it shares with other forms) saves 1.0 min a day for about +24 pages.
- With the card: 22 look-ups and 4.6 min a day, against 36 and 7.3. That is 37 % less handling for about +21 pages.

**3. The ribbons are assigned by section, not by use.**
- Ribbons 8–9 (Psalter 3 and 4), 2 (Commons) and 5 (Hymns) are opened fewer than 1.4 times a day.
- Ribbon 4 (Proper of Time) is opened 18 times a day.
- Used freely, the same ten ribbons would need 22 look-ups instead of 36.
- A cheap step towards that is giving ribbon 9 to the end of the Ordinary: 30 look-ups. Once the card exists, it adds almost nothing.

**4. Printing the weekly psalter and its hymns in the four-week cycle**, as the four-volume breviary does, removes most of the antiphon ↔ psalm turning.
- Psalms: 1.4 min a day for about +207 pages.
- Hymns: 0.3 min a day for about +248 pages.
- Everything together: 14 look-ups and 2.8 min a day, for about +477 pages.
- This works against the one-volume goal and the psalter-with-margin-notes design, so it is a trade-off for the owner rather than a fix.

**5. Looking up each new text once is unavoidable** when every text is printed once: each hour's psalms, hymn and readings are new pages.
- That floor is about 20 look-ups a day for the full Office, fewer for Lauds, Vespers and Compline only.
